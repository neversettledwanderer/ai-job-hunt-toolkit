# Odyssey Plan Exercise

From Bill Burnett and Dave Evans' "Designing Your Life" (Stanford). Helps clarify direction when the user is unsure what they are optimizing for, questioning whether their current target is the right one, or stuck between options.

## The Exercise

Design three radically different five-year plans for your life and career. Not three variations on the same theme. Three genuinely different paths.

### Plan 1: The Current Path
Where the user ends up if the current strategy works. The [your target career path].
- What role do you have in 5 years?
- What company or type of company?
- What does your day-to-day look like?
- What are you known for?
- How does this feel?

### Plan 2: The Alternate Path
What the user would do if Plan 1 disappeared entirely. Their target roles don't exist, their current career path isn't an option. What else?
- Could be: an adjacent discipline at a higher level, consulting/advisory, startup founder, teaching, something completely different
- The constraint: it has to be something the user would actually find fulfilling
- What role do you have in 5 years?
- What does your day-to-day look like?
- How does this feel compared to Plan 1?

### Plan 3: The Wild Card
What the user would do if money and reputation didn't matter. No financial constraints, nobody's watching.
- This is often the most revealing plan
- It surfaces values and desires that get suppressed by "practical" thinking
- What role do you have in 5 years?
- What does your day-to-day look like?
- What about this feels exciting or scary?

## How to Coach This Exercise

1. **Don't rush it.** This isn't a 10-minute exercise. Each plan deserves real thought. It's OK to spread this over multiple sessions.
2. **Push for specificity.** "I'd start a company" is not specific enough. What company? Doing what? With whom? Why?
3. **Watch for Plan 2 and Plan 3 converging.** If the alternate and wild card paths look similar, that's a strong signal about what the user actually wants.
4. **Watch for Plan 1 feeling like obligation.** If the user describes Plan 1 with duty language ("I should," "it makes sense") but Plan 2 or 3 with energy language ("I'd love to," "that would be amazing"), probe that.
5. **Look for elements that appear in multiple plans.** These are non-negotiable values or activities.
6. **Don't judge any plan.** The wild card is supposed to be wild.

## After the Exercise

- Compare the three plans. What overlaps? What's unique to each?
- Rate each plan on: confidence (can I do this?), engagement (am I excited?), coherence (does this fit my life?)
- Identify which elements from Plans 2 and 3 could be incorporated into Plan 1
- Use the results to sharpen targeting strategy: are there roles or companies that combine the best elements?

## When NOT to Use This

- When the user has clear direction and just needs help executing
- When the user is in the middle of an active interview process and needs tactical help
- When the user is feeling overwhelmed (this exercise adds complexity, not reduces it)
