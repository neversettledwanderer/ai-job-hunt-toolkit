# Energy Mapping

From Burnett and Evans' "Designing Your Life." Helps optimize how the user spends their job search time by identifying which activities energize vs. drain them.

## The Exercise

Track job search activities over 1-2 weeks. For each activity, rate on two axes:

**Engagement** (1-5): How absorbed were you? 1 = checking the clock, 5 = flow state
**Energy** (1-5): How did you feel after? 1 = drained and dreading more, 5 = energized and wanting more

### Activity Categories to Track

- Writing/customizing resumes
- Writing cover letters
- Searching for job postings
- Submitting applications
- Networking: reaching out to contacts
- Networking: having conversations
- Interview preparation
- Actual interviews
- Researching companies
- Working on portfolio/case studies
- Writing thought leadership content
- Contributing to AI product communities
- Informational interviews
- Salary/offer research
- Administrative tasks (tracking applications, updating spreadsheets)

### Recording Format

the user can track however is easiest. A simple list works:

```
Activity: Had an informational call with PM at [company]
Engagement: 4 (interesting conversation, learned a lot)
Energy: 5 (felt excited and motivated after)

Activity: Customized resume for [company]
Engagement: 2 (tedious, hard to stay focused)
Energy: 2 (felt drained, avoided the next one)
```

## How to Coach This Exercise

1. **Look for patterns, not individual data points.** One bad day of resume writing doesn't mean the user hates it. But if resume customization consistently drains them while conversations energize them, that's signal.

2. **High engagement + high energy = do more of this.** These activities are where the user is most effective and most motivated. Structure the search to maximize these.

3. **Low engagement + low energy = automate, delegate, or minimize.** If application form filling is consistently draining, that validates investing in the job-applicator agent and batch processing.

4. **High engagement + low energy = investigate.** These might be activities the user finds intellectually interesting but emotionally taxing. Interviews often fall here. The fix is usually recovery time, not avoidance.

5. **Low engagement + high energy = quick wins.** Activities that are easy and leave the user feeling good, even if they're not deeply engaging. Useful for building momentum on low-motivation days.

## After the Exercise

- Restructure the user's weekly job search routine to front-load high-energy activities
- Identify which low-energy activities can be delegated to agents (resume agent, application agent)
- Find ways to build more of the energizing activities into the routine
- Revisit periodically, energy patterns change as the search evolves

## When to Use This

- When the user feels burned out on the search
- When effort feels high but results feel low
- When deciding how to allocate limited time and energy
- After a major strategy shift (new activities may have different energy profiles)
