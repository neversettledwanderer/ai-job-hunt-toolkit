# Competitive Positioning Research

A methodology for researching how others have positioned themselves for similar roles, and using those findings to sharpen the user's positioning.

## When to Use

- When targeting a new company type or role level
- When current positioning isn't getting traction
- When the user wants to understand what "good" looks like in the market
- Before a major positioning overhaul

## Research Process

### Step 1: Find Comparable Profiles

Use WebSearch and WebFetch to find people who:
- Made a similar transition (from [YOUR_CURRENT_FIELD] to [YOUR_TARGET_FIELD])
- Hold the type of role the user is targeting
- Have non-traditional backgrounds for the target role (career switchers from various fields)

Search queries to try:
- "[target company] [target role title] LinkedIn"
- "[your current field] to [target field] transition"
- "non-traditional [target role] background"
- "[your field] to [target field] career change"
- "[target role title] former [your current title]"

### Step 2: Analyze Their Positioning

For each profile found, note:
- **Headline:** How do they describe themselves? Do they mention their non-traditional background?
- **About section:** What narrative do they tell? How do they bridge their past to their current role?
- **Experience framing:** How do they describe their pre-transition roles? What language do they use?
- **Skills emphasis:** What do they lead with?
- **Content/thought leadership:** Are they writing or speaking about [TARGET_FIELD] topics?

### Step 3: Identify Patterns

Look for:
- Common positioning strategies that appear across multiple successful transitioners
- Language patterns that bridge [YOUR_CURRENT_FIELD] to [YOUR_TARGET_FIELD]
- How they handle the "years of [TARGET_FIELD] experience" question
- Whether they downplay or highlight their previous-field background
- What they do differently from traditional candidates

### Step 4: Compare to the user's Current Positioning

- Where is the user's positioning stronger than the people researched?
- Where is it weaker?
- What language or framing could the user adopt?
- What are they doing that the user isn't (thought leadership, advisory roles, certifications)?

### Step 5: Generate Recommendations

Specific, actionable recommendations for how the user could adjust their:
- LinkedIn headline and about section
- Resume profile paragraph
- Elevator pitch / networking introduction
- Content strategy (if applicable)

## How to Coach This Exercise

- This is research-heavy. The coach does most of the searching and synthesizing, then presents findings to the user for discussion.
- Don't overwhelm with 20 profiles. 3-5 well-analyzed examples are more useful.
- Focus on what's transferable to the user's specific situation. Not every positioning strategy will apply.
- Be honest about what the market rewards even if it's not what the user wants to hear.
- Update this research periodically. The market evolves.
