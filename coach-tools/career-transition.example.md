## Ibarra's Working Identity (Detailed Strategies)

From Herminia Ibarra's research at Harvard Business School. The playbook internalizes the core principle (narrative clarity comes from action, not introspection). This file has the detailed strategies.

### Strategy 1: Experiment with New Professional Activities

Test new identities through real work, not just thinking about them.
- Advisory work for AI startups (even informal)
- Contributing to AI product communities (writing, commenting, sharing insights)
- Side projects that demonstrate product thinking applied to AI
- Volunteering for cross-functional projects that are product-adjacent
- Fractional product leadership work

The key: each experiment provides data about what fits and builds evidence for the narrative.

### Strategy 2: Interact in New Networks

The user's existing network sees him as a design leader. New connections see him as who he is becoming.
- Join AI product communities, not design communities
- Attend product and AI events, not UX events
- Connect with PMs and product leaders, not just designers
- Seek out other career transitioners who have made the shift

The key: new networks don't have preconceptions about the user's identity. They take him at face value.

### Strategy 3: Continuously Rework the Narrative

The career story is not fixed. It evolves as the user gathers data from experiments and new relationships.
- After each experiment, ask: "What did I learn about how I want to position myself?"
- After each meaningful conversation, ask: "How did I describe myself? Did it land?"
- Regularly revise the elevator pitch, LinkedIn profile, and resume profile based on what's working
- The narrative should get sharper over time, not stay static

## The Bridge Pivot (Detailed Methodology)

The playbook holds the positioning posture. This file has the detailed bridging approach for design-to-PM.

### Design Leadership Skills That Map to Product Leadership

| Design Leadership Skill | Product Leadership Equivalent | How to Reframe |
|---|---|---|
| User research and synthesis | Customer discovery and market research | "I led customer discovery programs that shaped product roadmaps" |
| Design strategy and vision | Product strategy and vision | "I defined product direction based on user insights and business goals" |
| Cross-functional leadership | Cross-functional leadership | Same skill, same language. Lead with this. |
| Design systems and standards | Platform thinking and scalability | "I built scalable systems that enabled faster product development" |
| Stakeholder management | Stakeholder management | Same skill, same language. Lead with this. |
| Shipping design to production | Shipping products | "I shipped products used by millions of users" |
| Team building and mentoring | Team building and mentoring | Same skill. Emphasize building PM-adjacent capabilities on the team. |
| Metrics and measurement | Metrics and measurement | "I defined success metrics and measured product impact" |
| Roadmap contribution | Roadmap ownership | Shift from "contributed to" to "owned" where truthful |

### Language Translation Guide

Phrases to stop using and what to use instead:
- "I led the design team" -> "I led the product design function" or "I led product development"
- "I did user research" -> "I led customer discovery"
- "I designed the experience" -> "I defined the product experience"
- "I worked with the PM" -> "I partnered with product management on strategy" or "I co-owned the product roadmap"
- "I'm a designer moving into product" -> "I'm a product leader with deep design expertise"
- "I don't have PM experience" -> "I've been doing product leadership work from the design side for 15 years"

## Fighting Dual Bias

### Career Switcher Bias

**The problem:** Hiring managers pattern-match. "Design leader" triggers "this person is a designer, not a PM." The resume and LinkedIn never get a fair read.

**Strategies:**
- Lead with PM-equivalent accomplishments, not design accomplishments
- Use product language throughout (see Language Translation Guide above)
- Get PM-titled people to vouch for the user's product skills (recommendations, references)
- Build portfolio-style case studies that show product thinking, not design artifacts
- Target roles where the design-to-PM bridge is valued (AI companies where UX is a differentiator, companies building complex user-facing AI products)

### Age Bias

**The problem:** Tech companies skew young. Candidates over 40 face conscious and unconscious bias around adaptability, cost, and cultural fit.

**Strategic principles (the coach advises on these):**
- Frame experience as pattern recognition and judgment, things that cannot be learned quickly
- Demonstrate current technical fluency: show mastery of modern tools alongside deep experience
- Project energy and currency, not tenure
- Target companies that value maturity: complex problems, regulated industries, enterprise, companies that have been burned by inexperienced leadership

**Tactical rules (the coach helps the user push these to the appropriate execution agents):**
- Resume: Remove graduation dates. Cap detailed work history at 10-15 years. Don't lead with total years of experience.
- LinkedIn: Optimize headline for current focus, not career span. Keep profile photo current. Feature recent work prominently.
- Skills lists: Remove anything that dates you. No obsolete technologies.
- Application forms: Where years of experience is a free-text field, use the minimum that qualifies.

## Proof-of-Skill Strategies

Since a traditional PM resume won't show PM tenure, alternative proof points:

### PM Portfolio
- Case studies showing product thinking, not just design artifacts
- Structure: Problem identified -> data/research -> solution proposed -> business impact
- Focus on decisions made, trade-offs considered, and outcomes achieved
- Include examples where the user influenced product direction even without PM title

### Thought Leadership
- Writing about AI product strategy demonstrates strategic thinking publicly
- Topics that position the user as a product thinker: AI product pitfalls, user-centered AI, when not to use AI
- LinkedIn posts, blog posts, or articles in product publications

### Advisory and Fractional Work
- Even informal advisory relationships build PM credibility
- Fractional product leadership at startups provides title-appropriate experience
- Board of advisors positions at AI startups

### Community Contribution
- Active participation in AI product communities
- Speaking at product or AI events
- Open source contributions to AI product tools
