# Bridge Pivot: Transferable Skills Mapping

A hands-on exercise for mapping your existing skills to your target role's requirements. Use when preparing positioning for a specific role, or when you feel like you don't have the "right" experience.

## The Core Idea

A bridge pivot identifies skills that remain constant across domains and uses them as the bridge to the new role. You are not starting over. You are applying a proven toolkit to a new problem set.

## The Exercise

### Step 1: Extract the Target Role's Core Requirements

Read the job description or research the role type if no specific JD exists. Break requirements into categories:
- **Technical fluency** (understanding domain concepts, working with technical teams)
- **Strategic thinking** (roadmap, prioritization, business impact)
- **Cross-functional leadership** (working across multiple functions)
- **User/customer focus** (research, feedback loops, product-market fit)
- **Execution** (shipping products, managing timelines, removing blockers)
- **Communication** (stakeholder management, executive presentations, writing)

### Step 2: Map Your Experience to Each Requirement

For each category, identify specific examples from your career that demonstrate the skill. Draw from MASTER_BULLETS if available. Be concrete.

Example mapping structure:
| Requirement | Your Experience | Strength of Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic thinking | [Specific examples from your career] | Strong / Moderate / Gap |
| Technical fluency | [Specific examples from your career] | Strong / Moderate / Gap |
| Cross-functional leadership | [Specific examples from your career] | Strong / Moderate / Gap |
| [Domain-specific requirement] | [Specific examples from your career] | Strong / Moderate / Gap |

### Step 3: Identify Gaps and Strategies

For any category where evidence is weak:
- Is this actually required, or just listed? Many "requirements" are flexible.
- Can you reframe existing experience to cover this?
- Do you need to build proof points? Side project, advisory work, content.
- Is this a dealbreaker or a "nice to have"?

### Step 4: Write the Bridge Statement

One paragraph that connects your background to the target role. Structure:

"[What you bring] + [how it applies to this role] + [what makes this combination uniquely valuable]"

The bridge statement should convey: your background is not a weakness, it is a differentiated strength for this specific role.

## How to Coach This Exercise

- Don't let the user undersell their experience. If they say "I've never done X," probe: "Have you ever made a decision that directly affected X? Have you influenced X even without owning it?"
- Watch for the "imposter gap": the user may have done the work but never had the title. The skill is real even if the job title didn't match.
- Use the mapping to prepare for specific interviews, not just as a general exercise.
- The bridge statement should evolve. Different roles need different bridges.
