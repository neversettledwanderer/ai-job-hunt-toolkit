# Bridge Pivot: Transferable Skills Mapping (Design-to-PM Example)

A hands-on exercise for mapping the user's existing skills to product leadership requirements. Use when preparing positioning for a specific role, or when the user feels like he doesn't have the "right" experience.

## The Core Idea

A bridge pivot identifies skills that remain constant across domains and uses them as the bridge to the new role. The user is not starting over. He is applying a proven toolkit to a new problem set.

## The Exercise

### Step 1: Extract the Target Role's Core Requirements

Read the job description (or research the role type if no specific JD). Break requirements into categories:
- **Technical fluency** (understanding AI/ML concepts, working with engineering teams)
- **Strategic thinking** (roadmap, prioritization, business impact)
- **Cross-functional leadership** (working across eng, design, sales, marketing)
- **User/customer focus** (research, feedback loops, product-market fit)
- **Execution** (shipping products, managing timelines, removing blockers)
- **Communication** (stakeholder management, executive presentations, writing)

### Step 2: Map the User's Experience to Each Requirement

For each category, identify specific examples from the user's career that demonstrate the skill. Draw from MASTER_BULLETS if needed. Be concrete.

Example mapping:
| Requirement | The User's Experience | Strength of Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic thinking | Owned product roadmap at [PREVIOUS_COMPANY_1], built design strategy at [PREVIOUS_COMPANY_2] | Strong |
| Technical fluency | AI degree, 8 years as software engineer, shipped AI products at [PREVIOUS_COMPANY_1] | Strong |
| Cross-functional leadership | Led cross-functional teams at every company for 15+ years | Strong |
| P&L ownership | Less direct evidence, but influenced revenue through product decisions | Gap to address |

### Step 3: Identify Gaps and Strategies

For any category where evidence is weak:
- Is this actually required, or just listed? (Many "requirements" are flexible)
- Can the user reframe existing experience to cover this?
- Does he need to build proof points? (Side project, advisory work, content)
- Is this a dealbreaker or a "nice to have"?

### Step 4: Write the Bridge Statement

One paragraph that connects the user's background to the target role. Structure:

"[What the user brings] + [how it applies to this role] + [what makes this combination uniquely valuable]"

Example: "I bring 17 years of building and scaling product organizations, with deep technical foundations in AI and a track record of shipping products that users adopt. My design leadership background means I approach product strategy from the user's perspective first, which is exactly what AI products need to avoid being technically impressive but practically unusable."

## How to Coach This Exercise

- Don't let the user undersell his experience. If he says "I've never done P&L," probe: "Have you ever made a decision that directly affected revenue? Have you prioritized features based on business impact?"
- Watch for the "imposter gap": the user may have done the work but never had the title. The skill is real even if the job title didn't match.
- Use the mapping to prepare for specific interviews, not just as a general exercise.
- The bridge statement should evolve. Different roles need different bridges.
